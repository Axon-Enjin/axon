Your instinct on multi-branch mid-market is the right one, and it's worth being explicit about *why* — because the reason tells you which verticals and countries to pick.

Multi-branch works because **pain multiplies by location count while the buying decision stays single**. A leaky process that costs ₱40k/month at one branch costs ₱480k/month across twelve. But you're still selling to one COO or owner, not a procurement committee. That ratio — large quantifiable loss, short decision chain — is the actual thing you're hunting. Enterprise has the loss but a nine-month sales cycle. Single-location SMBs have the short cycle but the loss is too small to justify your fee.

## Fix one thing in your criteria first

"Doesn't care spending money" is the wrong filter, and if you optimize for it you'll attract bad clients. People who are loose with money are loose with *your* invoice too — they're chaotic, they change scope, they pay late.

What you actually want is the opposite: someone who cares *intensely* about money and is therefore capable of doing the arithmetic that makes your fee obviously correct. A CFO who scrutinizes every peso is your best buyer, because ₱800k against ₱6M of annual leakage is a decision she can make in one meeting.

Second refinement: loss aversion only converts when the loss is **already conscious**. If you have to teach them the leak exists, you're funding an education campaign — expensive, slow, and half of them will "think about it" forever. Target companies where someone is already visibly bleeding: they've built an elaborate spreadsheet to cope, they've *hired a person* whose entire job is manual reconciliation, they've already gotten a quote from someone else. Those are the ones where the pain is priced in their head before you walk in.

## Geography

**Australia is your strongest beachhead.** Timezone is nearly identical (2 hours), so you can do real-time standups — this alone eliminates the biggest offshore objection. Australian labor costs are high enough that local agencies bill somewhere in the AUD 150–250/hr range, which means your reference price is enormous. Australian mid-market is genuinely underserved because local agencies chase enterprise and government. And critically, Australian businesses already have deep cultural familiarity with Philippine outsourcing — you're not the first offshore vendor they've considered, which removes an entire trust hurdle. Add New Zealand as a low-cost extension once you have Australian logos.

**Philippines domestic is your portfolio and cash-flow engine, not your profit center.** Lower deal sizes, more relationship-driven, longer collection times. But: you can meet clients in person, close on referral, and — most importantly — build the case studies that make Australian sales possible. Don't dismiss it, just don't expect it to fund growth. There is real mid-market here: clinic and dental groups, dealership networks, pharmacy chains, food franchise operators, lending and pawnshop networks, private school groups, logistics operators.

**The US is the biggest prize and the wrong first move.** Twelve-to-fifteen hour timezone gap, saturated with agencies, buyers who need more credibility signals than you currently have, and higher legal/insurance expectations. Revisit at eight to ten strong case studies.

**Middle East (UAE, Saudi) is a real option people overlook** — four to five hours behind you, enormous multi-branch retail and clinic groups, willingness to spend. But it's relationship-gated; cold outbound mostly fails. Only viable if you have a personal connection who can introduce you.

**Skip UK, Canada, and Europe for now** — timezone pain without the compensating advantages Australia gives you.

## The vertical decision matters more than the country

Here's the part that connects to your pricing question: **value pricing is only possible with domain depth.** If every project is a different industry, every build is bespoke, you can't estimate confidently, you reuse nothing, and you're forced back into selling hours. If you've done nine dental groups, you can walk into the tenth and tell them their no-show rate before they mention it, quote a fixed fee without fear, and reuse 60% of the codebase. That's where your margin lives.

So pick one vertical. The best candidates share a trait: **the loss is arithmetic, not opinion.**

Strongest categories for that, in the Australian context:

- **Field service and trades groups** — HVAC, plumbing, pest control, electrical, with multiple crews and vans. Leakage is pure math: unbilled jobs, scheduling gaps, van inventory shrink, quote-to-cash delay. Private equity has been rolling these up, which means acquirers who need systems.
- **Allied health and clinic groups** — dental, physio, veterinary, aesthetics. No-shows, lapsed recalls, claim rejections. Every one of those has a peso figure attached, and Australian clinic groups have been consolidating fast.
- **Regulated care services** — aged care, disability, childcare. This is the purest loss-aversion play that exists, because non-compliance isn't opportunity cost, it's fines and licence risk. Fear of a specific penalty converts faster than hope of a gain, every time. Higher complexity, but also the highest willingness to pay.
- **Property management and equipment rental groups** — arrears, utilization gaps, asset loss. Again, all countable.

Avoid: startups, anything pre-revenue, government procurement, and any client whose quantified annual loss is less than about five times your fee. Below that multiple, the ROI argument stops being obvious and you're back to negotiating on price.

## Qualification filters worth writing down

Revenue roughly AUD 5M–50M. Five to fifty locations. Has an operations manager or COO but no CTO. Currently paying for three or more SaaS tools that don't talk to each other. Recently opened or acquired a location — growth strains systems and unlocks budget simultaneously. Operates under an audit or compliance regime.

## Two things to prepare before you sell into Australia

**Local presence signals.** A .com.au domain, an Australian phone number, and Australian references matter more than they should. Consider whether a local partner or reseller arrangement is worth the margin split for your first few deals.

**Data residency.** Australian clients — health and care sectors especially — will ask where data is hosted, and Privacy Act obligations plus state-level health records rules often mean it needs to sit in an Australian region. Have that answer ready and priced in. Being caught flat-footed on this reads as amateur.

One caution on pricing there: don't undercut to 20% of local rates. Mid-market buyers read a very low price as a quality signal in the wrong direction. Somewhere around 50–65% of local agency pricing is the sweet spot — clearly compelling, still credible. (Verify current Australian agency rates before you set numbers; those figures move.)

*Zilch.*

This is less about **which country** and more about **which economic profile**.

You are looking for clients that have four characteristics:

1. **They lose money every day because of operational inefficiencies.**
2. **They have authority and budget to solve those problems.**
3. **The cost of doing nothing is greater than your proposal.**
4. **Software is a strategic investment, not an expense.**

Those four matter more than whether they're in Manila, Sydney, or Singapore.

## I wouldn't start internationally.

Many founders think international automatically means bigger contracts.

It also means:

* longer sales cycles
* stronger competitors
* higher expectations
* legal and tax considerations
* more difficult trust-building without a portfolio

Axon Enjin is still building its reputation. Your first goal isn't maximizing contract size. It's building a repeatable sales engine and collecting case studies.

If you can repeatedly close ₱500,000 to ₱2,000,000 projects in the Philippines, you'll have a much stronger position when expanding abroad.

## Your ideal customer is not "SMEs."

That's too broad.

Instead, target businesses that have already proven they can grow but are now constrained by operations.

Think of companies with:

* 30 to 500 employees
* multiple departments
* multiple branches
* repetitive manual workflows
* spreadsheets everywhere
* increasing coordination problems
* a founder who feels the business is becoming harder to manage

That's where software creates measurable value.

# The positioning I would build for Axon Enjin

Don't become a software house.

Become an **Operations Transformation Partner**.

You don't sell dashboards.

You don't sell AI.

You don't sell mobile apps.

You sell outcomes like:

* Reduce operational costs.
* Eliminate manual work.
* Improve executive visibility.
* Accelerate decision-making.
* Enable confident scaling.

When those outcomes are clear, the conversation shifts from **"How much does your software cost?"** to **"How much are we losing by waiting?"**

That is where pricing power begins.

The companies that write the largest checks are rarely buying software. They're buying certainty that a painful, expensive problem will stop costing them money. That should be the category Axon Enjin competes in.
